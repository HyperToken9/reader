# 15 — Where does equation *content* come from?

Type: research
Status: resolved
Blocked by: —

## Question

Surfaced by [[13]], which chose re-typeset equations as the first HTML-layer feature and then found nothing in the pipeline produces the equation itself.

The gap is specific. [[09]] found equation glyphs extract as **C0 control characters** — the Δ in `Δp/Δt` arrives as U+0003 — so the text layer yields no usable maths. [[12]]'s layout model yields a **rectangle and a label** (`display_formula`, `inline_formula`), not content. Between "here is a box containing an equation" and "here is the equation" sits a model nobody has priced.

Establish, for the serious candidates — **PP-FormulaNet** (the natural fit, same PaddleOCR family as [[12]]'s detector), **UniMERNet**, **Texify**, **pix2tex/LaTeX-OCR**, and whatever else is current:

- **What it outputs** — LaTeX, MathML, or both? MathML renders natively in a browser; LaTeX needs KaTeX or MathJax, which is another dependency but a well-understood one.
- **How it runs.** ONNX-exportable and usable from `onnxruntime-node`, the way [[12]]'s detector turned out to be? Or Python-only? This is the same question that decided [[12]], and the answer matters just as much for [[08]].
- **Cost per equation** — latency on CPU, and model size. Note the access pattern is favourable: equations are a small fraction of a page and only need recognising when the reader reaches them, so this can be lazy and cached in a way full-page OCR cannot.
- **Accuracy on this corpus**, especially Millington, which is the equation-dense book and the one whose Δ glyphs are already known to be broken. Vendor benchmarks are on clean rendered formulae; a scanned-quality textbook crop is harder.
- **Licence**, code and weights separately. [[12]] found this is where most of the field disqualifies itself.

Then answer the question [[13]] needs: **is re-typeset equations a viable first HTML-layer feature, or does it cost a third model too many?** If the answer is no, [[13]] falls back to per-paragraph controls, which need only region boxes and no new model.

Note the second-order prize, and do not overreach for it: an equation recovered as LaTeX or MathML could in principle be *spoken* rather than skipped, which is the "real equation speech" item the map lists as fog. That is a much harder problem — reading maths aloud well is its own field — and it is not this ticket. Mention only whether the output would make it possible.

## Answer

**Yes — re-typeset *display* equations is a viable first HTML-layer feature, and it is the cheapest of the three models on this map.** Full findings: [research/15-formula-recognition.md](../research/15-formula-recognition.md). The winner is not the one this ticket expected. `breezedeus/pix2text-mfr` — **MIT code and MIT weights**, a TrOCR encoder/decoder, **113 MB of ONNX** — runs under **`onnxruntime-node` with no Python, no PaddlePaddle and no wrapper package**, in about fifty lines of hand-written greedy decode. On Millington p60, the exact page [[09]] flagged, it returned `v = \operatorname*{\lim}_{\Delta t \to 0}\frac{\Delta p}{\Delta t}` — **every Δ correct**, where the text layer supplies literal U+0003. That is the ticket answered in one line: the recogniser reads what the text layer destroyed.

**Measured on this corpus, not read about.** I reused [[12]]'s stack unchanged to get the boxes: 18 Millington pages at 200 dpi, layout model at ~610 ms/page, then each formula box converted to PDF points, padded 3 pt and **re-rendered from the PDF at 300 dpi** (cropping the 200-dpi raster would throw away resolution the recogniser wants — in the app this clip comes from a PDF.js `render()` with a transform). That yielded 25 `display_formula` and 27 `inline_formula` crops. Graded by opening every crop against the model's output: **pix2text-mfr is exactly right on 21/25 display equations and 25/25 parse in KaTeX**, at **495 ms median** on CPU. `PP-FormulaNet_plus-S` (Apache-2.0 both halves) scored **19/25 exact, 23/25 parseable** at 509 ms. Their errors are *disjoint* — pix2text-mfr's three failures are all diacritic slips in stacked column vectors, PP-FormulaNet's four are all token-level LaTeX corruption — so at least one is right on **24 of 25**. Both degrade only on matrices; **18 of the 19 non-matrix regions are correct in both models simultaneously**.

**The runtime question inverts [[12]], and I proved it rather than inferring it.** PP-FormulaNet was the natural fit — same family, same vocabulary, same lineage as the detector — and `paddle2onnx` PR #1523 claims to export it. It does export: a single 334 MB file, one image input, **the whole autoregressive decode as an in-graph ONNX `Loop`**, which is architecturally *better* than a three-file transformers-style export. The file loads in ORT and then **dies on execution at `Loop.0 → If.3 → Identity.669` with a rank-0/rank-1 mismatch — identically in `onnxruntime-node@1.24.3` and Python `onnxruntime==1.23.2`, at opset 19 and opset 16**, having warned of exactly this during export. I fed it PaddleX's own preprocessing tensors specifically so a mistake of mine could not be mistaken for a runtime bug. **PP-FormulaNet is Python-only in practice today.** Since pix2text-mfr scores higher on this corpus *and* runs natively in Node, there is no reason to pay a sidecar for it — which keeps [[08]]'s surface unchanged by this ticket.

**Output format is a non-question: take LaTeX and get MathML for free.** KaTeX 0.18.5 (MIT, already a well-understood dependency) renders every recovered string to MathML in-process at a **median 527 bytes** per equation. MathJax is not needed. Better, KaTeX's `throwOnError` doubles as a **free runtime gate** — it caught two of PP-FormulaNet's four hard failures before anything could reach the screen. It is a floor and not a score, though: PP-FormulaNet's `Iboldsymbol{=}\left[…` parses cleanly and renders the literal letters "Iboldsymbol".

**Scope it to `display_formula` only, and that is the most important line here.** All 27 `inline_formula` regions produced garbage from both models — **and the fault is the box, not the recogniser.** Opening those crops shows ragged rectangles that begin mid-word in the surrounding prose, swallow the following comma and parenthesis, and clip ascenders (`e v = ṗ),`). [[12]] treated the inline classes as annotations rather than containers and never needed them tight; this ticket needs them tight and they are not. Recovering inline maths would need a box-refinement pass first — and it is not urgent, because inline maths is **not the broken case**. An inline `ṗ` inside a spoken sentence is a pronunciation problem; a display equation is a *silent hole*, which is precisely why [[13]] picked this feature.

**Cost has the best shape on this map.** ~500 ms per equation, **14.0 s for all 25 display equations in the whole sample**, ~2.0 s to re-typeset the densest page in the book, 113 MB of weights (roughly half [[12]]'s 203 MiB layout model), 326 MB peak node RSS. And unlike layout analysis, which is a tax on **every** page, this is a charge on a **rare event**: 11 of the 18 sampled Millington pages contain no equations at all, and [[12]]'s 40-page sweep found only 11 display formulas across three books. It is also idempotent and permanently cacheable by (document, page, region box). So: **lazy, one page ahead, cached, never on document open** — three or four equations resolve in the time the TTS spends on the paragraph above them.

**The hazard is confident nonsense, and it changes one of [[12]]'s conclusions.** [[12]] §2 found the layout model mislabels Code Book p41's raster cipher-alphabet table as `display_formula`, and correctly said it "costs us nothing" because the region holds no text items. **That reasoning does not survive contact with this ticket.** I cropped those regions and the recogniser did not refuse — it emitted well-formed, KaTeX-renderable, entirely wrong LaTeX (`\mathrm{IIain~alphibet}\quad\textrm{abcdelghijklmnopqrstuvwxyz}`), one of them running to the 256-token cap at 4.0 s. A misclassification that was free in [[12]] is not free once something gets *rendered* on top of it. Three cheap mitigations, all of which [[13]]'s existing rules already point at: **never occlude** — offer the re-typeset equation beside/above the region or on demand, so the reader can see it disagree with the real page; **gate on KaTeX parse**; and **gate on token-cap-hit and region aspect**, since both false positives were far outside the shape distribution of real display equations.

**Licence-wise this is the cleanest corner of the field, and most of it still disqualifies itself.** Texify's weights are **CC BY-NC-SA 4.0** and Nougat's are **CC BY-NC 4.0** — both non-commercial, out on the same grounds [[12]] ruled out LayoutLMv3. (Also worth flagging: Surya's README now quotes a **$2M** ceiling where [[12]] §7 recorded $5M from `MODEL_LICENSE` — the direction of travel is down.) The single most attractive model in the field, **Texo** — 20M parameters, distilled from PP-FormulaNet-S, ONNX at 54+26 MB, transformers.js-ready, with MathML and Typst output — is **AGPL-3.0 on both code and weights** and therefore unusable. UniMERNet's weights are cleanly Apache-2.0 but it is 441 MB even for tiny, PyTorch-only first-party, and PaddleOCR's own table places it *behind* PP-FormulaNet-S at 32× the CPU latency. That leaves pix2text-mfr and PP-FormulaNet as the only permissive-end-to-end candidates, and only one of them runs where we already are. **One caveat to carry forward: pix2text-mfr's weights were last modified 2024-05-05** (the parent MIT repo is active, pushed 2026-08-23) — re-check before a real build, with `torvexlabs/unimernet-onnx` as the same-shaped fallback if it has rotted.

**On the second-order prize, strictly as the ticket asked**: the output makes spoken maths *possible* — MathML is the input format of MathJax's Speech Rule Engine (Apache-2.0), and §7 shows MathML is already in hand at 527 bytes an equation. That is the entire claim. Reading maths aloud well is its own field and stays fog on the map. **[[13]] does not need its per-paragraph-controls fallback**: build the equation feature, `display_formula` only, additive and non-occluding, KaTeX-gated, lazy and cached.
